## What This Is

**Win.Win AI Blind Spot Guide** helps readers notice what AI may not receive, cannot infer safely, or should not be asked to handle alone.

An **AI Blind Spot** is a condition where an AI-supported output can appear clear, complete, balanced or confident while AI cannot reliably see, hold, judge, verify, authorise or be accountable for something a responsible person must address.

**If you remember one idea:** AI can sound convincing while something important is still missing, unverified, unauthorised, or dependent on human judgement.

If you later recognise that kind of situation but cannot remember the exact risk or solution, treat it as a possible **AI Blind Spot** and return to this guide. Use [[AI Blind Spot Risk Library]] to understand and name the risk, then [[AI Blind Spot Control Map]] to find relevant Controls, Methods, or Accountable Routes.

This guide sits beside the **Win.Win AI Use Case Guide**.

```text
Win.Win AI Use Case Guide
= what task is being done, what risks apply, what review or escalation is needed.

Win.Win AI Blind Spot Guide
= what AI may be blind to, and what deeper checks or controls may be needed.
```

A useful companion question is:

```text
What do people know or use implicitly that has not been put into the source packet, prompt, or review basis?
```

Unstated context is any relevant context that is absent from, incomplete in, or not adequately represented in the task basis. It may be carried by people, available in approved records, held in both, or of unclear source.

Experienced people may use accumulated context automatically: repeated-task learning, tacit judgement, organisational memory, local and relational awareness, risk appetite, preferences, and timing sensitivity. AI receives only the task basis and other information actually made available. The person may also be incomplete, wrong, unauthorised, or biased.

This guide helps readers identify the AI Blind Spot risk, consult the Control Map, and select the relevant recognised Control or Controls before entering Control-specific guidance.

**Unstated Context Control** is the current detailed Control, but it is not the universal first Control. Its most developed guidance concerns identifying and safely eliciting unstated context. Related current-task guidance covers approved-source retrieval and extraction, representability, safe conversion, human-only handling, verification, accountable or specialist review, stopping, and escalation.

## How This Guide Is Organised

This guide is organised through a Practical Layer and a Reference Layer. The layer names describe how readers use the material, not whether one layer is more important than the other.

| Layer | Purpose | What it contains | When to use it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Practical Layer** | Helps readers apply this guide to a real AI-supported task. | [[Start Here]], [[AI Blind Spot Review Card]], [[Unstated Context Control]], [[Elicitation Techniques]], [[Organisational Context Handling]], [[Source Packet Add-On]], [[When Questions Are Not Enough]], [[Organisational Memory Habit]], and the templates. | Start here to identify the risk, consult the Control Map, select relevant Controls, and then use only the practical guidance, Methods, standing checks, or escalation steps required by the task. |
| **Reference Layer** | Explains the operating map, risks, Controls, Methods, Accountable Routes, terminology, scope, and authoring rules that support consistent use of the Practical Layer. | [[How To Use This Guide]], [[Guide Scope And Positioning]], [[AI Blind Spot Control Map]], [[AI Blind Spot Risk Library]], [[Glossary]], and [[Author and Editor Guide]]. | Consult this layer when a Blind Spot, Control, Method, Accountable Route, term, scope boundary, adaptation, or maintenance question needs more explanation. |

New readers do not need to read every reference file before using this guide. Start with the Practical Layer, then use the Reference Layer when the review needs deeper explanation.

## What This Guide Does Not Cover

This guide does not make AI accountable, authorised, locally situated, professionally experienced, or able to carry human consequences.

This guide does not transfer responsibility for decisions, approvals, verification, professional judgement, or consequences to AI.

This guide does not turn a prompt, source packet, elicitation answer, checklist, or AI-generated explanation into proof that an output is correct, complete, authorised, safe, or fit for use.

This guide does not replace:

- independent verification;
- source-owner checking;
- authority-owner sign-off;
- decision-owner review;
- recipient-impact review;
- bilingual/register review;
- operational-owner review;
- professional or specialist review;
- escalation for legal, financial, HR, privacy, regulatory, safeguarding, counselling, pastoral, theological, or other specialist matters.

This guide can help reviewers identify gaps, ask better questions, identify suitable Controls and Methods, and use Accountable Routes responsibly. It cannot remove the need for accountable human judgement.
