# Start Here

## 1. Purpose and Scope

Use this page as the **quick first-use entry point** to the Win.Win AI Blind Spot Guide. It gives a short route from a real AI-supported task to the relevant AI Blind Spot risk, recognised Control or Controls, available Methods, Accountable Routes, and a responsible review decision.

Use [[How To Use This Guide]] when you need to understand how the files in this guide work together. Use [[Guide Scope And Positioning]] when you need this guide’s scope and boundaries. Use [[AI Blind Spot Control Map]] when you need the canonical Controls, Methods, and Accountable Routes related to a risk.

**Begin with an AI-supported task and intended use that are clear enough to review. If the task or intended use is still unclear, use the Win.Win AI Use Case Guide first.**

**Key terms for first use**

| Term | Quick meaning |
|---|---|
| **AI Blind Spot** | AI can sound convincing while something important that a responsible person needs to notice, judge, verify, authorise, or decide is still missing or unresolved. |
| **AI Blind Spot risk** | A named way an AI Blind Spot may affect the task or output. If you cannot name the exact risk yet, start with **AI Blind Spot** and narrow it later. |
| **Control** | A recognised safeguard used to reduce or manage an AI Blind Spot risk. |
| **Method** | A practical way to carry out or support a Control. |
| **Accountable Route** | A person, role, reviewer, owner, specialist, or approved process to involve when responsibility, authority, competence, or expertise is needed. |
| **Task basis** | The information actually available for the AI-supported task and review, such as the source packet, prompt, approved material, and current decision or review basis. |

These are quick orientation cues, not competing canonical definitions. You do not need to read or memorise the whole [[Glossary]] before starting. Use the Glossary when you need a precise term meaning, [[AI Blind Spot Risk Library]] for canonical risk definitions, and [[AI Blind Spot Control Map]] for canonical Controls, Methods, and Accountable Routes.

This page is intentionally brief. It does not require every AI Blind Spot risk to begin with Unstated Context Control. Use the detailed unstated-context sequence only when **Unstated Context Control** is relevant.

## 2. Quick Start Steps

### 2.1 Step 1 — Name the possible AI Blind Spot risk

Use this question:

```text
What might AI be missing here that a responsible human reviewer should not miss?
```

Use [[AI Blind Spot Risk Library]] to identify the closest canonical risk. More than one risk may apply. If you recognise that something important may be missing or unresolved but cannot yet name the specific risk, start with **AI Blind Spot** and narrow it after opening the Risk Library.

---

### 2.2 Step 2 — Select the relevant Control or Controls

Use [[AI Blind Spot Control Map]] after identifying the risk.

Use this question:

```text
What recognised safeguard or safeguards are relevant to this risk in this task?
```

One risk may relate to several Controls, and one Control may address several risks. Do not assume that Unstated Context Control is the first or primary Control for every risk.

For example:
- a self-validation concern may primarily require **Verification Control** — check the point against a sufficiently independent approved source, tool, record, or suitably accountable reviewer rather than relying on the same AI output;
- pressure to reduce justified caution may primarily require **Challenge Handling Control** — keep a justified warning, uncertainty, or safeguard in place unless reliable new evidence or an authoritative basis supports changing it.

---

### 2.3 Step 3 — Use relevant guidance and Methods

For each selected Control, use the detailed guidance or canonical Methods that are actually relevant and available.

The Control Map identifies recognised Methods. A Method supports a Control; it does not replace the Control objective, accountable judgement, authority, or decision.

If a recognised Control is defined but its full method is not provided in this guide, use the Control definition and relevant Accountable Route. Do not treat this guide as a complete specialist framework.

---

### 2.4 Step 4 — If Unstated Context Control is relevant, use its detailed sequence

Use [[Unstated Context Control]] when relevant context may be absent from, incomplete in, or not adequately represented in the task basis.

In brief:

1. identify what may be missing;
2. identify the likely source — people, approved records, both, or unclear;
3. assess whether the context can be represented clearly and safely enough for AI use;
4. use [[Elicitation Techniques]] only when a suitable person may carry relevant context implicitly and a focused question is safe and permitted; and
5. choose the minimum safe handling needed for the current task.

Do not force sensitive, restricted, unreliable, or non-permitted context into AI. Use the detailed Control page or the [[AI Blind Spot Review Card]] when you need the full current-task handling choices.

---

### 2.5 Step 5 — Use an Accountable Route where required

Use the Accountable Routes defined in [[AI Blind Spot Control Map]] when responsibility, authority, competence, independence, specialist expertise, approval, or escalation must move to another person or approved process.

Before choosing **Approve** or **Correct then approve**, check whether:
- **reviewer standing** is clear — whether the reviewer is suitably placed to perform the required review; and
- **approval standing** is clear — whether the person or body has the authority and accountability to approve the output for its intended use.

Use [[Templates/Reviewer Standing Check]] if either is unclear.

---

### 2.6 Step 6 — Decide

Choose one:

```text
Approve
Correct then approve
Stop and escalate
```

A good review does not always end in approval. Sometimes the safest and most responsible answer is to stop and route the task to someone with the right authority, expertise, independence, or standing.

---

### 2.7 Step 7 — Preserve only what should not be lost

Preservation is a separate decision. If the review surfaced a safe, reliable, reusable lesson, use [[Organisational Memory Habit]] or [[Templates/Exception Note Template]] to decide whether it should be kept through an approved owner and location.

Do not preserve information merely because it may be useful later. Keep it only when it is necessary, permitted, sufficiently reliable, safe, reusable, and appropriate to retain.

**What to remember after first use**

```text
AI can look right while something important is still missing or unresolved.
AI Blind Spot risk → Control(s) → Method(s) or guidance → Accountable Route where required → decision
```

Even if you later forget the exact risk name or mitigation, recognising **“this may be an AI Blind Spot”** is enough to return to the **Win.Win AI Blind Spot Guide**. Use the Risk Library to understand the risk and the Control Map to find relevant ways to mitigate it.
