# When Questions Are Not Enough

## 1. Purpose and Scope

Use this page when **better questions are not enough** because the relevant context is sensitive, restricted, political, deliberately unspoken, outside the reviewer’s standing, unsafe to elicit, or dependent on a person or process with particular authority, permission, trust, competence, or responsibility.

The page helps the reviewer recognise signals that questioning should stop and the matter should move through an appropriate Accountable Route, approved source, restricted process, or escalation path.

It is not a complete specialist-escalation, safeguarding, HR, legal, privacy, pastoral, counselling, regulatory, or professional-practice framework. It helps identify when those routes are needed rather than replacing them.

---

## 2. What “standing” means

A reviewer has standing when they have enough:

- role
- trust
- context
- authority
- competence
- permission
- accountability

A person may be careful and well-intentioned but still not be the right person to approve the output.

Standing is the reviewer’s practical ability to answer, challenge, approve, correct, or escalate the matter safely and authoritatively.

It may also depend on access to the relevant source or record, knowledge of the local context, intended-reader perspective, professional or specialist competence, ability to disagree without a real or perceived penalty, and permission to handle the context.

---

## 3. When a person may not be able to answer safely

Better wording does not solve hierarchy, fear of consequence, lack of permission, lack of trust, lack of authority, or an inappropriate question.

Hesitation, delay, discomfort, vagueness, or redirection are not proof that information is being withheld. Do not press a person merely to obtain context for AI.

Safe and critical answering may require an approved process, suitable standing, and enough trust for the person to disagree or say that the question should not be answered here.

Relevant historical or relational context may be spread across several people. Do not assume one person’s account is complete.

If more than one perspective may be needed, route the matter to an accountable owner or approved process that has authority to gather and handle those perspectives appropriately.

Do not instruct ordinary reviewers to conduct informal investigations.

---

## 4. Signals that routing is needed

Route or escalate when the task involves:

- reorganisation or reporting-line changes
- role appointments, role removal, or comparative treatment
- staff, volunteer, member, or stakeholder conflict
- a person or group likely to feel blamed, exposed, or compared
- a sensitive incident not fully documented
- safeguarding or vulnerable-person concerns
- private history known only to a few people
- communication that may affect trust or reputation
- policy, eligibility, entitlement, refusal, or approval
- anything the reviewer would be uncomfortable explaining later

Also route rather than keep asking when:

- the reviewer cannot safely disagree;
- the reviewer is fresh to the draft but lacks relevant context;
- the reviewer knows the context but lacks authority or permission;
- the question would pressure someone to disclose sensitive context merely for AI use;
- the issue is legal, financial, HR, privacy, safeguarding, counselling, pastoral, theological, regulatory, professional, or authority-dependent;
- more than one perspective may be required through an approved process.

---

## 5. What not to do

Do not ask AI to guess sensitive context.

Do not ask AI to decide whether a politically sensitive message is safe.

Do not rely on AI to identify every issue that requires standing.

Do not:

- keep asking sharper questions merely to get more material for AI;
- treat discomfort or hesitation as evidence;
- force a person to disclose sensitive context;
- ask a reviewer to approve a matter outside their standing;
- create an informal investigation process;
- paste sensitive backstory into AI because it seems relevant.

---

## 6. What to do instead

```text
1. Pause routine drafting or review.
2. Name why the task may require standing.
3. Route it to an accountable owner or suitably placed reviewer.
4. If no suitable internal owner or reviewer exists, do not rely on the output for high-consequence use.
5. Seek suitable external qualified support, narrow the task, or stop the task.
6. Keep sensitive details out of ordinary AI prompts and shared notes.
7. Record only what is safe and necessary in the proper restricted place.
```

Use [[Templates/Reviewer Standing Check]] if reviewer standing is unclear.

Additional routes may include:

- narrowing the question to what the person can safely answer;
- retrieving an approved record and extracting the relevant part;
- keeping the matter human-only;
- verifying through a sufficiently independent approved source, tool, record, or suitably accountable reviewer;
  An owner confirmation may support verification only if the owner has suitable knowledge, authority, and accountability. A responsible owner is not automatically an independent verifier.
- routing to an accountable, qualified, or specialist reviewer;
- stopping and escalating before further AI use;
- not using or recording the context.

---

## 7. Practical rule

If the task depends on trust, authority, private history, conflict, or specialist judgement, a better question may not be enough. Use [[Templates/Reviewer Standing Check]] and escalate when needed.

When a person cannot safely or authoritatively answer, the control is not a better question. The control is the right route.
