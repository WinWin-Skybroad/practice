## 1. Purpose

AI tools offer genuine benefits for efficiency, communication, and the quality of many work tasks. Used well, they can free up time for the work that matters. Used without appropriate boundaries, they can cause harm — including data privacy breaches, factual errors in client communications, confidentiality failures, and reputational or regulatory risk.

This policy exists to help our staff and co-workers:

- **Use AI wisely** — within clear and agreed boundaries
- **Protect privacy** — of our clients, members, and the people we serve
- **Preserve integrity** — of our communications and professional reputation
- **Keep professional judgment human** — decisions affecting people's rights, employment, or welfare belong to accountable human staff
- **Meet legal obligations** — under Hong Kong's Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance (Cap. 486)

This policy does not prohibit the use of AI. It establishes the boundaries that allow AI to be used in a manner consistent with our values, our legal obligations, and our duty of care to the people we serve.

## 3. Our Governing Principles

All AI use within this organisation is governed by the following principles:

| Principle | What it means in practice |
|---|---|
| **Human review is mandatory** | Every AI output that is used in business must be reviewed and verified by a responsible employee before use. Speed is never a reason to skip this step. |
| **Client data is protected** | Client personal data, confidential information, and unpublished business data are never entered into public AI tools. |
| **Accuracy over speed** | AI-assisted content sent to clients or used in business decisions represents our organisation. Accuracy matters more than efficiency. We do not use AI output without reading it. |
| **Accuracy is the employee's responsibility** | The employee who uses or sends AI-assisted content is responsible for its accuracy — regardless of how it was produced. "The AI wrote it" is not a valid professional defence. |
| **Approved tools only** | Only AI tools on the organisation's approved list may be used for business tasks, unless prior written approval has been obtained from the Policy Owner. |
| **PDPO compliance is non-negotiable** | All AI use must comply with the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance (Cap. 486) of Hong Kong. |

## 6. Prohibited Use Cases — The Red Lines

The following uses are **not permitted** under any circumstances. These 'red lines' exist to protect our organisational integrity:

- [ ] **Client personal data in public AI tools** — Client names, HKID numbers, contact details, financial details, health information, or any personal data must never be entered into a public or personal AI tool.
- [ ] **Confidential business information in unapproved tools** — Trade secrets, unpublished financial data, client contracts, pricing strategies, or proprietary business information must not be entered into any unapproved AI tool.
- [ ] **Automated client communications** — AI must not send any communication to a client or external party without per-message human review and approval.
- [ ] **AI-assisted decisions about individuals** — Hiring decisions, disciplinary matters, performance assessments, or credit/eligibility assessments must not be made or recommended by AI without human judgment and accountability.
- [ ] **Legal or compliance documents without review** — Legal documents, compliance certifications, regulatory submissions, and tax filings must not be generated by AI without qualified human review and sign-off.
- [ ] **Generating misleading content** — AI must not be used to fabricate quotations, misrepresent capabilities or credentials, or create content that deceives clients or partners.
- [ ] **Unapproved tools** — AI tools not on the approved list in Section 4 must not be used for any business purpose without prior written approval from the Policy Owner.

## 7. Required Safeguards for All Approved Uses

The following safeguards apply to all approved AI use:

- [ ] **Human review** — every AI output shared with or affecting any external party must be reviewed and approved by a named responsible person before use
- [ ] **No sensitive data** — no personal data, HR data, or confidential documents are entered into a public AI tool
- [ ] **PDPO compliance** — where AI tools process any personal data, PDPO Data Protection Principle 3 compliance must be assessed first
- [ ] **TC readability** — where TC documents are used, they must be tested for machine-readability before AI processing
- [ ] **Source verification** — all facts, figures, citations, and claims in AI output are verified against primary sources before use
- [ ] **Specialist review** — any AI-assisted content with legal, compliance, or regulatory implications is reviewed by a qualified professional

## 8. Data Protection and Privacy — PDPO Compliance

All AI use must comply with the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance (Cap. 486) of Hong Kong. The following rules are mandatory:

| PDPO Requirement | How we comply in AI use |
|---|---|
| **DPP 1 — No personal data in public AI tools** | Client or employee personal data must not be entered into any publicly accessible AI tool. This includes names, contact details, HKID numbers, financial information, and health data. |
| **DPP 3 — Use limitation** | Personal data collected for one business purpose (e.g., client onboarding) may not be processed through AI tools for a different purpose without the individual's knowledge or consent. |
| **DPP 4 — Data minimisation** | When using AI for drafting or analysis, use the minimum amount of data necessary. Anonymise or remove personal identifiers where possible. |
| **DPP 4 — Security of outputs** | AI-generated outputs containing any client or employee information must be handled with the same confidentiality standards as other business records. |
| **DPP 4 — Breach reporting** | Any accidental entry of personal data into a public AI tool must be reported to the Policy Owner immediately. |

> [!warning] If you are unsure whether a specific AI use complies with PDPO — do not proceed. Seek guidance from the Policy Owner or a qualified privacy professional before continuing.

### Data Classification — Before Entering Anything into AI

Before using any data in an AI tool, classify it using this table:

| Classification | Examples | May I use this in AI? |
|---|---|---|
| **Public** | Published information, marketing materials, public announcements | Generally yes — in approved tools only |
| **Internal** | Internal procedures, non-sensitive memos, general organisation information | Yes — in approved tools only |
| **Confidential** | Client data, contracts, financial details, business strategies, pricing | No — not in public AI tools |
| **Restricted / Sensitive** | Personal data (HKID, health, salary), passwords, legal matters, compliance files | Never |

**The Bulletin Board Rule:** Before entering any information into an AI tool, ask: would I be comfortable if this information appeared on a public notice board? If no — do not enter it.

## 9. Human Review and Quality Control

Human review is not optional. It is a hard requirement for all AI-assisted output.

| Output type | Required reviewer | Timing |
|---|---|---|
| Client-facing emails and communications | Sending employee | Before every send |
| Client reports, proposals, and presentations | Author + manager (for significant outputs) | Before delivery to client |
| Internal documents and notices | Sending employee | Before distribution |
| Meeting summaries and minutes | Meeting organiser or secretary | Before sharing |
| Financial data extraction | Finance team member | Before entering any record |
| Translated content | Bilingual employee or qualified reviewer | Before publishing or sending |
| Legal or compliance-adjacent content | Qualified professional | Before any use |

The reviewer is responsible for:

- **Read fully** — not just skimming
- **Verify all facts** — names, dates, and figures
- **Correct errors** — before the content is used
- **Take responsibility** — "The AI wrote it" is not an accepted explanation for an error in client-facing content

## 10. Shadow AI (Using Personal Tools for Work Tasks)

Shadow AI means using personal AI subscriptions for work tasks, bypassing organisation-approved tools.

- [ ] **Consumer terms only** — personal AI accounts are not enterprise-protected
- [ ] **No work data** — personal AI tools must not be used for tasks involving client data or confidential organisation information
- [ ] **Training risk** — personal accounts may use inputs for AI training; creates data residency, training, and confidentiality risks the organisation cannot control
- [ ] **Raise, don't bypass** — if better tools are needed, raise with your manager; do not work around the gap using personal accounts

---

## 11. The Training Opt-Out Requirement

Many AI tools use inputs for model training by default. When using any AI tool for organisation-related tasks:

- [ ] **Check default settings** — do your approved tools allow inputs to be used for training?
- [ ] **Opt out if possible** — opt out before using the tool for any business content
- [ ] **Do not use if no opt-out** — if opt-out is not available, do not use that tool for any content containing organisational information

This applies especially to free or personal AI accounts used for business tasks.

## 12. Reporting and Breach Management

Employees must report the following to the Policy Owner immediately:

- **Unreviewed output sent** — any AI output sent to a client or published before required human review
- **Personal data exposed** — any personal data accidentally entered into a public AI tool
- **Policy violation** — any AI use that may have violated this policy or applicable law
- **Client concern** — any client complaint or concern related to AI-generated content

Reports made in good faith will be treated without blame. The purpose of reporting is to identify and correct problems — not to punish employees who raise concerns.

Where a breach of PDPO may have occurred, the Policy Owner will determine whether notification to the Office of the Privacy Commissioner for Personal Data (PCPD) is required and manage that process.

---

## 13. If Something Goes Wrong

If AI produces an error that has already been sent to a client or used in a business decision:

1. **Tell manager** — immediately; do not try to quietly fix it alone
2. **Preserve the record** — keep what was sent and what the error was
3. **Assess impact** — who received it, what decisions it may have affected
4. **Correct promptly** — through the appropriate channel as quickly as possible
5. **Update the process** — review what safeguard should have caught this

## 14. Consequences of Non-Compliance

> [!note] Adapt to your organisation's disciplinary framework and employment arrangements. Volunteer or government organisations should seek HR or legal advice on the appropriate consequence language for their context.

## Appendix B — Quick Reference for Employees

**Before using AI for any work task, ask:**

- **Purpose** — Is this task appropriate for AI? Is the use case clearly defined?
- **Permission** — Is this tool approved? Is the data classified correctly? Is it safe to use in AI?
- **Proof** — Will a responsible person review this before it reaches a client or goes on record?

**Data classification at a glance:**

| Green — generally OK in approved tools | Red — never in public AI tools |
|---|---|
| Public information | Client personal data |
| Internal non-sensitive content | HR records, salary, performance data |
| Anonymised examples | Contracts, legal documents, confidential strategy |
