# AI Safety Checklist — Work

> [!info] Use case record:  
>  
> **Task:** (e.g. church newsletter drafting, meeting note summarisation)  
> **Date:**  
> **AI tool used:**  
> **Filled in by:**  

> [!quote] Why this matters at work:  
> Using AI at work creates responsibilities you may not have when using AI personally.  
> You are handling your organisation's information, your clients' trust, and your professional reputation.  
> "AI said it" is not a professional defence. You are responsible for what you submit.  
>  
> This checklist helps you use AI at work safely, within your organisation's rules, and in ways you can stand behind.  
> Use it every time you try a new AI tool or task.  

> Treat AI output as a draft engine, not a truth engine.  
> Every output needs a human reader before it reaches anyone or anything that matters.

**Who this is for:** Staff, contractors, and volunteers using AI tools in any work context.

**Important:** "Safer AI use" means reducing common risks. It does **not** mean guaranteed safety, accuracy, or compliance.

---

## 1. Know Your Organisation's Rules First

- [ ] **Know the policy** — does an AI usage policy exist?
- [ ] **Approved tools** — which AI tools are approved for work?
- [ ] **Allowed tasks** — which tasks may AI help with, and which are off-limits?
- [ ] **Who to ask** — who do I go to if unsure about a specific AI use?

> [!important] If your organisation has no AI policy yet:  
> Follow this checklist as your personal standard until a policy exists.  
> Raise with your manager that a policy is needed. You may share the AI Usage Policy template from Win.Win@Skybroad as a starting point.

---

## 2. Before Every Work Task (3P Check)

### P1 · Purpose — Is this the right task for AI at work?

- [ ] **Clear purpose?** — drafting, summarising, researching, or translating
- [ ] **Human reviews output** — before it affects anyone or anything
- [ ] **Not a decision** — I am not using AI to make a decision that belongs to a person
- [ ] **Within policy** — the use case fits what my organisation permits
- [ ] **Know the cost** — clear about what a wrong output would mean

> [!warning] AI is a drafting and support tool — not a decision-maker:  
> Final decisions about people, contracts, compliance, and client commitments must be made by a human.

---

### P2 · Permission — What data can I use with AI at work?

**Data classification — before entering anything into an AI tool:**

| Classification | Examples | May I use this in AI? |
|---|---|---|
| **Public** | Published policies, public announcements | Generally yes — in approved tools only |
| **Internal** | Internal memos, non-sensitive procedures | Yes — in approved tools only |
| **Confidential** | Client data, contracts, financial details, HR records | No — not in public AI tools |
| **Restricted / Sensitive** | Personal data (HKID, health, salary), passwords, legal matters, pastoral records | Never |

**NEVER put into a public or personal AI tool:**

- [ ] **Client personal data** — names, contacts, account numbers, personal records
- [ ] **HR records** — salary, performance data, employee information
- [ ] **Confidential contracts** — legal agreements, non-disclosure protected content
- [ ] **Confidential strategy** — business plans, financial forecasts, internal pricing
- [ ] **Marked confidential** — anything labelled Confidential, Restricted, or Internal
- [ ] **Credentials** — passwords, API keys, authentication tokens
- [ ] **Others' personal data** — name, ID, address, phone, email, health, finances
- [ ] **Sensitive HR/pastoral** — personnel matters, pastoral disclosures
- [ ] **Uncomfortable publicly?** — if I would not share it publicly, do not enter it

> [!note] The simple rule:  
> If you would not write it on a postcard and leave it on a public notice board, do not put it into a public AI tool.  
> This applies to client data, internal financials, strategic plans, and personal details.

**Ask before using:**

- [ ] **Personal data involved?** — check PDPO compliance first
- [ ] **Client materials?** — confirm your organisation permits AI use on client work
- [ ] **Compliance documents?** — confirm the AI tool meets data handling requirements

> [!note] HK-specific — PDPO Data Protection Principle 3:  
> Personal data collected for one purpose cannot be used for a materially different purpose without consent.  
> Using a client's personal data in an AI tool may violate PDPO even if the data stays within your organisation.  
> When in doubt — leave personal data out.

---

### P3 · Proof — Can I verify, track, and be accountable for what AI produces?

- [ ] **Read full output** — before using it in any work context
- [ ] **Can stand behind it?** — comfortable if challenged
- [ ] **Verify claims** — facts, figures, citations, legal references checked
- [ ] **Expect errors** — AI can hallucinate confidently; I know this
- [ ] **Can explain it?** — able to tell my manager or client what AI helped with

> [!warning] "AI said it" is not an excuse:  
> You are responsible for work you submit. If AI produced an error you did not catch, the error is yours.  
> The employee who generates the content is the owner and is responsible for fact-checking and quality control.

#### Hallucination warning signs — verify before trusting

Watch for these in any AI output before using or sharing it:

- [ ] **Statistic with no source** — specific figure with no named origin: search the original before using
- [ ] **URL or reference** — check any link or citation actually exists and leads where it claims
- [ ] **Named individual** — AI may invent biographical details: verify from an official source
- [ ] **HK law or regulation** — HK-specific rules are frequently wrong in AI: verify on gov.hk or elegislation.gov.hk
- [ ] **100% confident tone** — no uncertainty or caveats in the output: a warning sign, not reassurance

## 3. Specific Workplace Situations

### Emailing clients or external parties
- [ ] **Read in full** — not just accepted and sent
- [ ] **Accurate details** — client names, figures, and commitments verified
- [ ] **Right tone** — appropriate for the relationship and context
- [ ] **No confidential data** — nothing sensitive was put into the AI prompt
- [ ] **Disclosure checked** — considered whether AI assistance must be disclosed per policy

### Summarising documents or reports
- [ ] **Accuracy checked** — summary verified against the original
- [ ] **Key figures preserved** — decisions, figures, thresholds, and caveats not lost
- [ ] **No restricted content** — document did not contain confidential or restricted information

### Using AI for research or analysis
- [ ] **Sources verified** — claims and statistics checked against original sources
- [ ] **No compliance reliance** — not relying on AI for legal, regulatory, or compliance conclusions
- [ ] **Marked AI-assisted** — output clearly labelled if sharing internally

### Meeting notes and minutes
- [ ] **Accuracy reviewed** — notes checked before circulating
- [ ] **Correct attribution** — names and action owners correctly assigned
- [ ] **No sensitive content** — meeting did not involve HR, legal, or pastoral matters
- [ ] **Human summarised** — sensitive discussions written up by a person, not AI

### Translations
- [ ] **Bilingual reviewer** — qualified reviewer checked before external use
- [ ] **HK terminology** — legal, financial, or technical terms verified against official HK sources
- [ ] **Tone unchanged** — AI did not strengthen, soften, or alter beyond the source material

## 4. Agentic and Automated AI (Higher Risk)

> The following two sections apply when your organisation uses automated AI tools or when personal AI accounts are involved.  
>  
> If neither applies to your current task, skip to Section 6.

If your organisation is using AI tools that can take actions — send emails, update records, book meetings, submit forms:

- [ ] **Know the permissions** — what actions is the AI permitted to take on my behalf?
- [ ] **Know the approvals** — which actions require my explicit approval first?
- [ ] **Human review gate** — a review step exists before any externally-facing action
- [ ] **Can pause or reverse** — a way to stop or undo the AI's actions if something goes wrong
- [ ] **Actions are logged** — AI actions are recorded and auditable

> [!warning] Agentic AI can act before you notice:  
> If an AI tool can send messages, submit forms, or update data, assume it will — set clear boundaries before enabling it.

---

## 5. Shadow AI (Using Personal Tools for Work)

Shadow AI means using personal AI subscriptions (ChatGPT personal, Claude free account, etc.) for work tasks.

- [ ] **Consumer terms only** — personal AI accounts are not enterprise-protected
- [ ] **No work data** — not using personal AI tools for client data or confidential information
- [ ] **Training risk** — personal accounts may use inputs for AI training
- [ ] **Raise, don't bypass** — if better tools are needed, raise with manager; don't work around it

---

## 6. Output Archiving and Accountability

For high-stakes or decision-support tasks:

- [ ] **Logs saved** — prompt and output saved to internal records
- [ ] **Full responsibility** — prepared to take professional responsibility as if I wrote it myself
- [ ] **Second review** — if it affects a client relationship or formal decision, a second person has reviewed it

---

## 7. If Something Goes Wrong

If AI has produced an error that has already been sent, used, or submitted:

- **Tell manager** — immediately; do not try to fix it alone
- **Preserve the record** — keep what was sent and what the error was
- **Assess impact** — who received it, what decisions it may have affected
- **Correct it** — through the appropriate channel as quickly as possible
- **Learn from it** — what review step should have caught this?

If personal data may have been accidentally exposed through an AI tool — this may trigger a PDPO notification obligation. Raise it with your manager or compliance contact immediately.

## 8. Quick Reference

| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| **Unsure if AI is allowed** | Ask your manager before using AI |
| **About to paste client data** | Stop — use anonymised examples or a company-approved tool |
| **AI gave a wrong answer** | Do not use it; flag to your manager |
| **AI acted on your behalf** | Check what was sent; notify your manager if wrong |
| **No AI policy exists** | Use this checklist; flag that a policy is needed |
| **HK law or compliance** | Verify on official HK source; involve a solicitor |

---

## 9. Not Suitable for AI at Work — Always Use a Human

- **Employment decisions** — affecting pay, discipline, or employment status
- **Legal advice** — contract interpretation or compliance sign-off
- **Financial commitments** — approvals and financial decisions
- **Medical decisions** — clinical judgments
- **Client commitments** — without human review
- **Pastoral care** — counselling or sensitive personal conversations

---

## 10. High-Risk Categories — Extra Caution Always

Some output types carry higher risk regardless of how simple the task appears. Verify these before using or sharing:

| Category | Risk | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| **Scripture / theology** | Plausible but doctrinally wrong content | Verify in the Bible directly; consult your pastor |
| **Legal / regulatory** | HK-specific law frequently wrong in AI | Verify on gov.hk, elegislation.gov.hk, or solicitor |
| **Financial / investment** | May be outdated or inapplicable to HK | Verify with a licensed advisor or SFC/HKMA source |
| **Named individuals** | AI may invent biographical details | Verify from the person's official profile or contact |
| **Recent events** | AI training has a cutoff date | Search a live news source |
| **Church / org communications** | May affect relationships or reputation | Human pastoral or managerial review before sending |

---

## 11. One-Minute Final Check

Before you use, send, or publish anything AI helped create:

- **Purpose** — Was AI the right tool for this task?
- **Permission** — Did I keep sensitive data out of the prompt?
- **Proof** — Have I read this, checked the key claims, and can I stand behind it?

If all three are yes — you have done the minimum responsible thing.
