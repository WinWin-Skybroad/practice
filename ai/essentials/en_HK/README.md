## What This Is

**Win.Win AI Essentials** is a self-service collection of practical tools to help individuals, churches, nonprofits, and SMEs in Hong Kong **use AI more safely** and **avoid failed AI projects**.

This kit is suitable for:
- Hong Kong SMEs exploring AI for the first time
- Churches and nonprofits evaluating AI for administration and communication
- Team leaders and managers who want to make better decisions about AI adoption
- Anyone who wants a structured starting point for safer AI use

**Using these materials does not guarantee safe, compliant, or successful AI outcomes. All decisions remain the responsibility of the reader.**

These materials are shared as general information and insight for independent use. They do not constitute consulting services, legal advice, compliance certification, or technical implementation guidance.

## How To Use This Kit

This kit does not tell you to avoid using AI.  
It helps you decide where AI is useful, where it needs checking, and where it must never act alone.  
Use AI for speed and drafting. Keep humans responsible for judgement, approval, and care.

> [!note] What AI actually is:  
> Today’s AI can analyse, summarise, compare, and draft at a level that genuinely impresses. What it cannot do is check whether its output is actually correct. It has no reliable way to confirm facts, no awareness of its own errors, and no way to tell you when it is guessing.  
> The more confident and well-written the output, the easier it is to miss a serious mistake.  
>
> **Use it to draft. Never let it decide.**  
> **Polished is not proof.**

AI is a powerful drafting tool — like a chainsaw, it can do in seconds what used to take much longer. But you would not use a chainsaw without a safety guard or without watching what you are cutting.

Safe AI use is not leaving the tool in the box. It is being the operator — knowing exactly what the tool can and cannot do — and always reading the output before it reaches anyone.

**The safety guard is you.**

**Automation bias** means over-trusting AI because it sounds confident, fast, or well-written. AI can produce content that seems plausible but is not suited to your congregation, your clients, or your specific situation. Human review is not a backup step — it is part of the work.

## Two Core Frameworks

Everything in this kit is built on two simple frameworks.

### 3P — Safer AI Use

Ask before every AI task:

| Check | Question |
|---|---|
| **P1 — Purpose** | Why are we using AI for this task? Is the purpose clear and appropriate? |
| **P2 — Permission** | What do our data and tools allow? Is this tool approved for this data? |
| **P3 — Proof** | Can we verify the output and show what happened? Is there a human reviewer? |

### 3R — AI Project Success

Ask before committing to any AI project:

| Check | Question |
|---|---|
| **R1 — Right Problem** | Is AI genuinely the right solution for this problem? |
| **R2 — Ready** | Are we ready across all four dimensions: data quality and permissions, organisational readiness for change, process design, and governance? |
| **R3 — Realistic Expectations** | Have costs, time, effort, and expected outcomes been honestly assessed? |

> [!quote] Teaching summary:  
> Before your everyday AI use — ask 3P.  
> Before you commit to an AI project — ask 3R.  
> If you cannot clearly answer all six questions, pause and think first.

## Four Hong Kong-Specific Reminders

These apply to every organisation in Hong Kong and run throughout these materials.

> [!warning] 1 — Check PDPO Before You Prompt:  
> Personal data collected for one purpose must not be used for AI processing without assessment — this is required by Hong Kong's Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance (PDPO, Cap. 486), specifically Data Protection Principle 3 (DPP3: data must only be used for the purpose it was collected for). When in doubt — leave personal data out. Check [www.pcpd.org.hk](https://www.pcpd.org.hk) for guidance.

> [!warning] 2 — Test Traditional Chinese Documents First:  
> Scanned Traditional Chinese documents may not be machine-readable by AI tools. Always paste a test section before processing any Traditional Chinese document at scale.

> [!warning] 3 — Human Review Before It Reaches Anyone:  
> No AI output may be sent to clients, congregation members, staff, or the public before a human has read it. This is the minimum standard of responsible use.

> [!warning] 4 — AI Cannot Replace Pastoral Care:  
> Pastoral counselling, spiritual direction, and theological authority require humans — always. These are permanently out of scope for AI use in any ministry context.

## Glossary

*A short reference for terms used in this kit.*

| Term | Plain-English meaning |
|---|---|
| **PDPO** | Hong Kong's Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance (Cap. 486) — the law that governs how personal data may be collected, stored, and used. |
| **DPP3** | Data Protection Principle 3 — the specific rule that says personal data collected for one purpose must not be used for a different purpose without the person's knowledge or consent. |
| **RAG** | A way to build AI that answers questions from your own documents rather than from its general training. Short for Retrieval-Augmented Generation. Requires technical setup. |
| **Shadow AI** | Using personal AI tools (such as a personal ChatGPT or Gemini account) for church or work tasks without the organisation's knowledge or approval — creating data risks that cannot be controlled. |
| **AI Agent / Agentic AI** | An AI tool that can take actions on its own — such as sending emails, updating records, or booking appointments — without requiring a human to approve each step. Different from standard AI, which only generates text for a human to review. |
