# Church Quick Start

You do not need to open every file before beginning. Pick the task closest to what you need today, open that page, and use the **How To Review This Output** section before approving any AI output.

These cards help you enter this guide quickly.

---

## 1. Read This First

Before using this quick start page, read [[Start Here]] first.

This quick start page helps you find the use cases that are most relevant to your organisation type. It is not a replacement for this guide’s main orientation.

[[Start Here]] explains how to use this guide, how to choose a use case, how to read each use-case page, and why every AI output still needs human review before it is sent, shared, approved, published, or acted on.

After reading [[Start Here]], return to this page and choose one practical task to begin with.

---

## 2. Five Tasks To Start With

### 2.1 Draft WhatsApp Broadcasts

Weekly announcements, event reminders, service notices, and ministry updates.

→ Open: [[Use Cases/Draft WhatsApp Broadcasts]]

**First check:** Does this message contain any personal information, implied commitment, wrong date, or statement that the person responsible for sending it has explicitly verified and approved — reading it as a recipient, not as the author?

---

### 2.2 Draft Event Planning Checklists

Christmas, Easter, retreat, fellowship, outreach, AGM, and other church events.

→ Open: [[Use Cases/Draft Event Planning Checklists]]

**First check:** Has someone with operational knowledge of this organisation — not just the checklist — confirmed that every site-specific, team-specific, booking, and approval step is present before this checklist is used for planning?

---

### 2.3 Draft Communications

Emails, newsletters, announcements, notices, bulletins, and social media posts.

→ Open: [[Use Cases/Draft Communications]]

**First check:** Has a responsible person checked every name, date, figure, audience, tone, and implied commitment against the approved brief before sending or publishing?

---

### 2.4 Produce Bilingual Versions

Traditional Chinese and English versions of bulletins, announcements, and service materials.

→ Open: [[Use Cases/Produce Bilingual Versions]]

**First check:** Has a qualified bilingual reviewer read the Traditional Chinese output alongside the English source, sentence by sentence — not just checking that the Chinese reads well, but confirming that the meaning, conditions, and commitments are identical in both versions?

---

### 2.5 Summarise Meeting Notes

Committee notes, ministry planning notes, action lists, and follow-up summaries.

→ Open: [[Use Cases/Summarise Meeting Notes]]

**First check:** Has someone who attended this meeting compared every decision, action item, action owner, deadline, deferred item, and unresolved question against the original notes?

---

## 3. One Rule

Read and check the AI output before sending, sharing, approving, or acting on it. AI may draft, summarise, translate, or structure; it must not become the final approver.
