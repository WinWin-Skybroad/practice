# Escalation Guide

## 1. Purpose and Scope

Escalation means stopping ordinary approval and asking the right accountable person to review before the output is used.

Escalation is not failure. It is a safety control.

The reviewer does not need to stop merely because a sensitive topic is mentioned. The reviewer may continue only if they are explicitly authorised, competent, properly informed, and following the approved process for that matter.

---

## 2. When Ordinary Review Is Not Enough

Escalate when the reviewer cannot safely approve the output within their role, evidence, authority, competence, or approved process.

Escalate when any of the following are true:

- The reviewer is not the right person to approve the topic.
- The reviewer lacks the source material, context, source access, domain knowledge, or approved process needed to verify the output.
- The output appears to make or imply an organisational decision that the reviewer is not authorised to approve.
- The output creates or implies a commitment, promise, instruction, approval, public statement, policy position, deadline, refund, payment, or obligation that the reviewer is not authorised or properly informed to confirm.
- The output affects legal, financial, HR, privacy, regulatory, safeguarding, pastoral, theological, medical, public, or other specialist matters, and the reviewer does not have the authority, competence, context, source access, or approved process needed to confirm safe use.
- The output affects personal data, confidential data, client data, member data, donor data, beneficiary data, vulnerable groups, complaints, crisis situations, or public reputation, and the reviewer does not have the authority, context, source access, or approved process needed to confirm safe use.
- The output contains uncertainty that cannot be resolved by checking the available source.
- The reviewer can edit the wording, but cannot confirm whether the substance is correct.

Escalation does not mean AI cannot assist. It means the output must not be approved, sent, published, acted on, or relied on until the right accountable person has reviewed it.

## 3. Escalation Routes

### 3.1 Who To Escalate To

| Issue                                                                             | Escalate To                                                                            |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Legal, regulatory, HR, or compliance issue                                        | Qualified legal, compliance, HR, or management reviewer                                |
| Personal data or confidentiality issue                                            | Privacy, data protection, or responsible organisational leader                         |
| Finance, grant, refund, payment, or funding issue                                 | Finance owner, grant owner, or authorised management reviewer                          |
| Pastoral counselling, theological advice, spiritual direction, or doctrinal issue | Appropriate human authority in the organisation; do not use AI output as the authority |
| Public-facing communication or reputation issue                                   | Responsible owner of the communication or organisation leader                          |
| Cybersecurity or tool approval issue                                              | IT, cybersecurity, or responsible system owner                                         |
| Source ownership or disputed source issue                                         | Source owner, record owner, meeting chairperson, policy owner, or responsible manager  |
| No suitable reviewer is available                                                 | Pause the task. Do not use the AI output as the final decision                         |

---

### 3.2 If Your Organisation Has No Specialist Staff

Small churches, nonprofits, and SMEs may not have in-house legal, HR, IT, cybersecurity, finance, or compliance staff. In that case, escalation means asking the most responsible and competent available person, not pretending a specialist exists.

Escalation may mean involving an internal leader, a board or committee member, an external adviser, a professional service provider, or pausing the task until a suitable reviewer is identified.

| Situation                                 | Practical escalation path for small organisations                                                                                                                             |
| ----------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Church or ministry matter                 | Pastor, elder, deacon board, ministry leader, church council, or an appointed reviewer                                                                                        |
| Nonprofit service or beneficiary matter   | Programme manager, case/service lead, board chairperson, responsible committee member, or external adviser                                                                    |
| SME legal, HR, or finance matter          | Business owner, director, manager, accountant, HR service provider, lawyer, or other qualified adviser                                                                        |
| Legal, compliance, or regulatory question | Pause the task. Do not proceed on AI output alone. Seek a qualified professional, pro bono legal service, governing body, or trusted external contact with relevant expertise |
| Data protection or confidentiality issue  | The person accountable for data in the organisation. If no one holds this role, treat the task as escalated until a responsible person is identified                          |
| Cybersecurity or tool approval issue      | IT provider, system owner, cybersecurity adviser, or responsible organisational leader                                                                                        |
| No suitable reviewer is available         | Pause the task. Do not use the AI output as the final decision                                                                                                                |

Escalation can be as simple as saying:

**“This may affect rights, money, reputation, personal data, pastoral or counselling matters, or official responsibility. I am not the right person to approve it alone.”**

When in doubt, stop. A delayed task is usually recoverable. An action taken on wrong AI output may not be.

---

## 4. Practical Stop Rule

> [!warning] Stop when safe approval is not possible
> Do not approve, send, publish, act on, or rely on the output when you cannot verify it or approve it within your role, evidence, authority, competence, or approved process.

- Pause the task when the AI output sounds confident but you cannot verify it.
- Pause the task when the output affects rights, obligations, care, money, reputation, or access to service and you cannot safely approve it within your role.
- Pause the task when the AI creates a claim, promise, decision, or conclusion that was not in the source material.
- Pause the task when the reviewer does not have authority, competence, source access, context, or approved process to approve the content.
- Pause the task when the tool or account has not been approved for the data involved.
