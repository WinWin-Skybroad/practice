# Pilot And Stop Rule Card

## 1. Purpose and Scope

A real pilot must be small enough to stop.

If nobody has defined what would pause the pilot, the pilot may become a quiet rollout before the organisation has learned whether the review process works.

---

## 2. Pilot Setup

Before piloting an AI use case, define:

- use case being piloted
- owner
- reviewer
- pilot period
- intended users
- source material allowed
- output destination
- success measure
- incident log location
- stop rule
- person authorised to pause the pilot

---

## 3. Stop Rule Examples

Pause and reassess if:

- a reviewer cannot complete the required review within the expected time
- output is used without review
- personal data or confidential information is entered into an unapproved tool
- a material factual error is found after approval
- the use case starts affecting a wider audience than planned
- the task begins to touch legal, HR, finance, privacy, regulatory, pastoral, theological, safeguarding, or other specialist matters
- the named owner or reviewer is unavailable and no backup is assigned

---

## 4. Pilot Review Questions

At the end of the pilot, ask:

1. Did the review method actually happen each time?
2. Which part of the workflow was skipped or unclear?
3. Were the source packets complete enough?
4. Was the reviewer matched to the risk?
5. Did volume make review thinner than expected?
6. Should the use case become approved, conditional, not yet, or stopped?

---

## 5. Practical Rule

A pilot without a stop rule is not a pilot. It is a small launch.
