# Reviewer Decision Card

## 1. Purpose and Scope

Use this card before any AI output is sent, published, relied on, or treated as final.

AI output is a draft or review aid. The final responsibility remains with the human user and the organisation.

---

## 2. How To Use This Reference

Use-case pages contain the task-specific review and escalation guidance for each AI task. Their **Reviewer decision** section is only a short decision gateway.

This card is the full reusable decision logic. Open it when the reviewer needs to decide whether the output can be approved as is, corrected then approved, or must stop and escalate.

Use the use-case page first, then use this card when the approval decision needs more explicit decision support.

---

## 3. Entry Structure Standard

Each reviewer-decision entry uses a small common structure so the reviewer can see the decision threshold without confusing editing authority with approval authority.

---

### 3.1 Mandatory Sections

#### When to choose this

State the conditions that must be true before the reviewer selects the decision.

---

### 3.2 Optional Sections

#### Examples

Use short examples where they clarify the difference between a correctable issue and a matter that must stop or escalate.

#### Next action

State the action that follows the decision where it is not already clear from the decision name and conditions.

#### Evidence to retain

Identify any source, correction, approval, escalation, or decision record that should be retained.

---

## 4. Reviewer Decisions

### 4.1 Approve As Is

#### When to choose this

Choose this only when all of the following are true:

- The output matches the source material.
- The reviewer has checked facts, names, dates, figures, caveats, and commitments.
- The output stays within the approved task, audience, and intended use.
- The reviewer has the role, context, source access, competence, authority, and approved process needed to approve the content.
- The output does not create a new decision, commitment, obligation, claim, public position, or sensitive disclosure beyond the reviewer’s remit.
- No important uncertainty remains.

---

### 4.2 Correct Then Approve

#### When to choose this

Choose this when all of the following are true:

- The issue is minor.
- The reviewer has authority and competence to correct it.
- The reviewer has the source material and context needed to verify the correction.
- The corrected version can be checked again before use.
- The correction does not require a new decision, commitment, specialist judgement, approved process, or approval from another accountable person.

#### Examples

- correcting a wrong date against the official event notice.
- restoring a missing caveat from the source document.
- replacing vague wording with the approved wording.
- removing an unsupported claim.
- clarifying wording without changing the substance, authority, process, or commitment of the message.

---

### 4.3 Stop And Escalate

#### When to choose this

Stop and escalate when the reviewer cannot safely approve the output within their role, evidence, authority, competence, or approved process.

The reviewer does not need to stop merely because a sensitive topic is mentioned. The reviewer may continue only if they are explicitly authorised, competent, properly informed, and following the approved process for that matter.

Escalate when any of the following are true:

- The reviewer is not the right person to approve the topic.
- The reviewer lacks the source material, context, source access, domain knowledge, or approved process needed to verify the output.
- The output appears to make or imply an organisational decision that the reviewer is not authorised to approve.
- The output creates or implies a commitment, promise, instruction, approval, public statement, policy position, deadline, refund, payment, or obligation that the reviewer is not authorised or properly informed to confirm.
- The output affects legal, financial, HR, privacy, regulatory, safeguarding, pastoral, theological, medical, public, or other specialist matters, and the reviewer does not have the authority, competence, context, source access, or approved process needed to confirm safe use.
- The output affects personal data, confidential data, client data, member data, donor data, beneficiary data, vulnerable groups, complaints, crisis situations, or public reputation, and the reviewer does not have the authority, context, source access, or approved process needed to confirm safe use.
- The output contains uncertainty that cannot be resolved by checking the available source.
- The reviewer can edit the wording, but cannot confirm whether the substance is correct.

Escalation does not mean AI cannot assist. It means the output must not be approved or used until the right accountable person has reviewed it.

---

## 5. One-Line Rule

> [!important] Approval depends on evidence and authority
> Do not approve because the output sounds good. Approve only when the output is accurate, appropriate, authorised, and reviewable by the person approving it.
