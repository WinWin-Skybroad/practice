# Start Here

The **Win.Win AI Use Case Guide** is a practical guide for using AI in everyday organisational work.

It is organised by use case because AI risk depends on what the user is trying to do: drafting a notice, summarising meeting notes, translating a document, preparing a grant application, researching a topic, or handling information that may involve personal data, money, authority, reputation, or sensitive relationships.

This guide has a special focus on **human review that actually works**: what a person should check before AI output is used, shared, approved, or acted on.

Treat AI output as a **draft, helper, or review aid**. It is not the final decision. A responsible person or organisation is still accountable for what is sent, approved, published, or acted on.

---

## 1. Who This Is For

This guide is for individuals, churches, nonprofits, and SMEs using AI for everyday work.

It is especially useful for people who choose, prepare, review, approve, or adapt AI-supported work in their own context. Readers may adapt examples, prompts, terminology, and workflow details, but should not remove the human-review, privacy, source-verification, authority, escalation, or accountability controls required by the task.

You do not need to be an AI expert to use this guide. Start with the task you are trying to complete, then use the relevant use-case page to decide what must be reviewed before the AI output reaches anyone else.

---

## 2. What This Guide Helps You Do

This guide helps you:

- choose the closest AI use case for a task
- understand what could go wrong in that use case
- decide whether AI is suitable for drafting, summarising, translating, structuring, or reviewing support
- use prompt patterns and safeguards carefully
- check AI output against sources, facts, authority, audience, and risk
- decide whether to approve, correct, or stop and escalate
- train reviewers using practical review patterns and failure modes
- adapt the use-case page structure for your own organisation

This guide does not assume that one review method fits every task. Different use cases need different review actions.

---

## 3. What This Guide Does Not Do

This guide does not provide legal advice, compliance certification, technical implementation guidance, financial advice, medical advice, counselling advice, theological advice, or pastoral counsel.

It does not tell users that AI output is safe because a prompt was used.

It does not replace qualified review where the task involves legal, HR, finance, personal data, safeguarding, counselling, theological or pastoral matters, regulatory, cybersecurity, public reputation, or official organisational matters.

It does not make AI the final approver. The final responsibility remains with the human user or organisation.


> [!important] Human review remains mandatory
> AI output is a draft or review aid. The responsible human must review it before it is sent, published, approved, relied on, or acted on.

---

## 4. How To Choose a Use Case

Start with the task, not the tool.

Ask: **what are you trying to use AI for?**

If you are drafting something for others to read, start with a communication use case.

If you are summarising notes or documents, start with a summarisation use case.

If you are translating or producing bilingual material, start with a bilingual use case.

If you are researching, checking confidence, or assessing reliability, start with a research or reliability use case.

If the task touches personal data, money, HR, law, safeguarding, counselling, theological or pastoral matters, public reputation, cybersecurity, or official commitments, read the relevant use case and the [[Escalation Guide]] before using the output.

If no use case fits well, do not force the task into the closest page. Use [[Use Case Page Template]] to define the task properly, or stop and ask a responsible reviewer.

---

## 5. How To Read a Use-Case Page

Each use-case page explains:

- what the AI task is
- when the use case may be appropriate
- what could go wrong
- what a human reviewer must check
- when the output can be approved, corrected, or escalated
- which related files in this guide may help

Do not treat a use-case page as permission to use AI automatically. Treat it as a structured way to decide whether AI is suitable, what safeguards are needed, and what review must happen before the output reaches another person.

When reviewing a use-case page, read these sections first:

- **What This Could Cost You** — understand the practical risk.
- **How To Review This Output** — use this to guide the task-specific review before approving, correcting, or escalating the AI output.
- **When To Escalate Instead of Approve** — check whether approval should stop because the task exceeds the current reviewer’s authority, expertise, evidence, approved process, or responsibility.
- **Other Mitigation** — check what safeguards are needed beyond prompting.
- **Related Win.Win AI Essentials Items** — follow these links when the task needs broader foundation guidance.

## 7. Five Tasks To Start With

You do not need to open every file before beginning. Pick the task closest to what you need today, open that page, and use the **How To Review This Output** section before approving any AI output.

These five starter use cases are common, practical, and reviewable.

---

### 7.1 Draft Communications

Emails, announcements, notices, bulletins, and event communications.

→ Open: [[Use Cases/Draft Communications]]

**First check:** Has a responsible person read this draft as if receiving it for the first time — checking every date, name, figure, and implied commitment — not just the overall tone?

---

### 7.2 Summarise Meeting Notes

Turn rough meeting notes into decisions, action items, and follow-up points.

→ Open: [[Use Cases/Summarise Meeting Notes]]

**First check:** Has someone who attended this meeting compared every decision, action item, and action owner in this summary against the original notes — with the original document open, not from memory?

---

### 7.3 Draft WhatsApp Broadcasts

WhatsApp or Telegram messages for updates and reminders.

→ Open: [[Use Cases/Draft WhatsApp Broadcasts]]

**First check:** Does this message contain any personal information, implied commitment, wrong date, or statement that the person responsible for sending it has explicitly verified and approved — reading it as a recipient, not as the author?

---

### 7.4 Produce Bilingual Versions

Traditional Chinese and English versions of the same communication.

→ Open: [[Use Cases/Produce Bilingual Versions]]

**First check:** Has a qualified bilingual reviewer read the Traditional Chinese output alongside the English source, sentence by sentence — not just checking that the Chinese reads well, but confirming that the meaning, conditions, and commitments are identical in both versions?

---

### 7.5 Summarise Documents

Summarise reports, board papers, contracts, or other documents.

→ Open: [[Use Cases/Summarise Documents]]

**First check:** Has someone checked this summary against the original document to confirm that no condition, deadline, exception, qualification, or limitation has been dropped, softened, or merged into a general statement?

---

## 8. One Rule

Read and check the AI output before sending, sharing, approving, or acting on it. AI may draft, summarise, translate, or structure; it must not become the final approver.
