# Draft Communications

## 1. What This Use Case Is

Use this page when you want AI to help draft or improve everyday organisational communications, including emails, newsletters, announcements, notices, bulletins, event communications, and social media posts.

- Common users: **Church, nonprofit, SME**.
- Approval level: **Generally approved**.
- Human review needed: **Yes — before sending or publishing**.
- Use the AI output to help you draft or check the work. Do not treat it as the final message until a responsible person has reviewed it.

---

## 2. What This Could Cost You

A communication can sound warm, clear, and ready to send while carrying an old date, wrong venue, incorrect name, unsupported figure, or unintended commitment from background notes. If the reviewer checks only tone and flow, the message may reach readers with an error that affects attendance, expectations, trust, or the organisation’s ability to deliver what was stated.

---

## 3. How To Review This Output

Use the checks below before approving the AI output. Do not approve it just because it reads well.

---

### 3.1 Review Setup

1. Open the source material and the draft side by side.
2. Check every date, venue, name, figure, deadline, and link in the draft against the source — not against memory.
3. Check every implied commitment, promise, apology, approval, position, or deadline. Ask whether the organisation has actually authorised it.
4. Read the draft as a recipient, not only as the writer. Writers read what they intended. Recipients read what is written.
5. Check whether the draft includes or hints at personal, confidential, pastoral, HR, financial, donor, client, beneficiary, or sensitive information that should not be shared.
6. Confirm that the tool, account, and workflow are approved for the source material used.

---

### 3.2 Before You Approve — One Check

> **Has a responsible person read this draft as if receiving it for the first time — checking every date, name, figure, venue, deadline, link, and implied commitment against the source, not just the overall tone?**

---

### 3.3 Risk-Specific Review Checks

These checks adapt the minimum review obligations in [[Human Review Risk Library]] to this task.

- **[[Human Review Risk Library#4.1 Low-Risk Assumption Trap|Low-Risk Assumption Trap]]:** Check every recipient name, date, time, venue, link, contact detail, figure, deadline, requested action, and implied promise or commitment against the approved brief and current source material, not memory. Correct or escalate any item the reviewer cannot confirm.
- **[[Human Review Risk Library#4.7 Prompt Over-Trust|Prompt Over-Trust]]:** Independently apply the source-and-fact, authority, privacy, recipient-impact, and approval checks required by this message. Do not approve it because the prompt is tested, approved, detailed, or apparently followed.
- **[[Human Review Risk Library#4.10 Instruction Drift|Instruction Drift]]:** Compare the final version after every revision round with the original purpose, approved facts, audience, tone, source-only boundary, privacy limits, prohibited claims, and approval boundary. Restore or escalate any requirement that was weakened or lost.
- **[[Human Review Risk Library#4.13 AI Blind Spot|AI Blind Spot]]:** Whether the output fits the real people, real situation, real authority, and real consequences behind the task, including tacit context the AI was not told and lived or professional judgement that cannot be fully reduced to a prompt.
  - **For this use case:** Check the draft against the actual recipients, relationship context, timing, recent events, sender authority, organisational position, and consequences of misunderstanding or acting on the message.
- **[[Human Review Risk Library#4.16 Recipient Impact Blindness|Recipient Impact Blindness]]:** Check who may be affected and whether the timing, tone, disclosure, requested action, or implied pressure could cause confusion, embarrassment, exclusion, distress, loss of trust, or reputational harm. Use an audience-aware reviewer where the impact is sensitive.

---

### 3.4 Reviewer Decision

After completing the task-specific review checks above, choose one outcome using [[Reviewer Decision Card]]:

- **Approve as is**
- **Correct then approve**
- **Stop and escalate**

Do not approve the output if the reviewer lacks the role, evidence, authority, competence, source access, context, or approved process needed for this task.

---

### 3.5 Review Methods for This Task

The review instructions below are customised for this task. They apply the reusable methods in [[Human Review Patterns]] to the actual sources, decisions, people, and consequences involved here.

- **General output review:** Check that each message fulfils the stated purpose, suits its channel and audience, contains the required action, and remains within the approved length and format.
  - Look for: Missing call to action, wrong audience, format drift, unclear next step, or a message that no longer answers the brief after revisions.
  - Timing: Before sending or publishing.
  - Reviewer: Communication owner or responsible sender.

- **Source and fact verification:** Verify every name, date, time, venue, amount, link, deadline, capacity, and factual claim against the approved source.
  - Look for: Routine details copied from memory or old event material, invented links, and unsupported claims.
  - Timing: Before sending or publishing.
  - Reviewer: Source owner or communication reviewer with access to the approved facts.

- **Human-grounded review:** Read the message as the actual recipients, considering timing, relationship, recent events, likely interpretation, and possible impact.
  - Look for: Wording that is technically clear but insensitive, alarming, misleading, or inappropriate for the real recipients.
  - Timing: Before final approval.
  - Reviewer: Audience-aware reviewer, programme owner, or person who knows the recipients and current context.

- **Scope, authority, and feasibility review:** Confirm that the sender is authorised to make every promise, commitment, apology, exception, policy statement, or call to action in the message.
  - Look for: Boundary softening, unofficial organisational voice, undeliverable commitments, or implied approval.
  - Timing: Before sending or publishing.
  - Reviewer: Authorised communication owner or decision owner.

## 4. When To Escalate Instead of Approve

- Escalate before use if the output affects legal, HR, financial, safeguarding, medical, regulatory, public-facing, or high-impact decisions and the reviewer does not have the authority, competence, context, source access, or approved process needed to confirm safe use.
- Escalate when a claim affects legal, financial, pastoral, theological, public, or external decision-making and the reviewer is not authorised or competent to confirm it.
- Escalate when wording concerns vulnerable groups, culture, crisis, complaints, or public reputation and the reviewer lacks the context, authority, or approved process to confirm safe use.
- Escalate when the output could bind the organisation, affect rights or obligations, or appear as official approval.
- Escalate before tool use when confidential data, third-party systems, financial processing, or automation is involved and the tool, account, reviewer, or process has not been approved for that use.

---

## 5. Before You Prompt

Before using AI for this task, check these basics:

- The purpose is clear.
- The input does not contain personal or confidential data that should not be entered into the tool.
- The tool, account, and workflow are approved for the material being used.
- A responsible person can check the AI output before it is used.
- The task does not ask AI to make the decision, provide professional advice, or act as the final authority.
- Confirm that this task fits the approval level: **Generally approved**.
- Confirm that the planned reviewer can complete the required human review: **Yes — before sending or publishing**.

---

## 6. AI Blind Spot Focus

**Risk focus:** Recipient Impact Blindness

**Prompt focus:** Before prompting, give the AI the intended audience, channel, purpose, relationship context, approved facts, sensitivity level, sender authority, timing, and what the message must not imply. Ask it to identify wording that could be misunderstood, sound stronger than intended, imply approval or authority, create an unintended commitment, appear insensitive, or confuse the reader.

**Human review focus:** The reviewer should read as the actual recipient, not only as the sender. Check whether the real audience, timing, relationship, organisational position, and context could make the message land differently from what the sender intended.

**Deeper Blind Spot review:** Follow the [[How To Use This Guide#6.1 Win.Win AI Blind Spot Guide|Win.Win AI Blind Spot Guide route]] when the message depends on unstated relationship history, recent events, internal tension, audience sensitivity, or someone’s likely reaction.

**Do not rely only on:** clear wording alone. A message can be grammatically clear but socially, organisationally, or practically misunderstood.

## 8. Main Risks

- **[[Risk Taxonomy#3.1 Accuracy / Hallucination|Accuracy / Hallucination]]: [[Human Review Risk Library#4.1 Low-Risk Assumption Trap|Low-Risk Assumption Trap]] (High)** — Drafting a newsletter, notice, or announcement feels routine. That familiarity is the risk. Because the task seems simple, reviewers check for obvious errors and approve quickly. The specific failures embedded in communications — wrong dates from pasted context, implied commitments not intended, personal details carried from notes — are precisely the ones that pass a quick read undetected. The incidents that cause the most damage in organisations are often not from tasks where everyone is careful. They are from familiar tasks where no one expects a problem.


- **[[Risk Taxonomy#3.1 Accuracy / Hallucination|Accuracy / Hallucination]]: [[Human Review Risk Library#4.7 Prompt Over-Trust|Prompt Over-Trust]] (Medium)** — Users or reviewers may treat a well-designed, tested, or approved prompt—and AI’s apparent compliance with it—as evidence that the message is safe, accurate, or ready to use. The required review may then be reduced or skipped even though the prompt cannot replace the review patterns, verification, authority checks, or escalation required by this task.

---

## 9. Other Risks To Watch

- **[[Risk Taxonomy#3.11 AI Blind Spot / Context Risks|AI Blind Spot / Context Risks]]: [[Human Review Risk Library#4.16 Recipient Impact Blindness|Recipient Impact Blindness]] (High)** — AI may write a message that is clear and polite but misses how real recipients will feel, misunderstand, or react based on timing, relationship, recent events, or audience expectations.
- **[[Risk Taxonomy#3.1 Accuracy / Hallucination|Accuracy / Hallucination]]: Format Inconsistency (Low)** — AI does not reliably replicate the exact format of previous newsletters. Reviewers check content but not layout fidelity, allowing format drift across issues to go undetected.
- **[[Risk Taxonomy#3.1 Accuracy / Hallucination|Accuracy / Hallucination]]: Session Memory Gap (Medium)** — AI has no memory of previous newsletters or communications. It cannot identify what is inconsistent across issues or catch a pattern break. Each session starts from zero.
- **[[Risk Taxonomy#3.7 Tone / Representation / Cultural Sensitivity|Tone / Representation / Cultural Sensitivity]]: Tone Drift (Medium)** — AI may gradually shift tone beyond what was requested. Tone drift is harder to detect than factual errors because it is subjective and gradual. Output may misrepresent the organisation’s voice.
- **[[Risk Taxonomy#3.6 Authority / Scope / Commitment|Authority / Scope / Commitment]]: Boundary Softening (High)** — AI may imply a commitment or soften a stated boundary that was not explicitly intended in the original notes. The output passes a fact-check but makes an unauthorised organisational commitment.
- **Pastoral / Theological / Scripture: Sensitive Data in Prompt (High)** — User may paste sensitive member information, confidential details, or pastoral content into the prompt. The prompt pattern itself cannot prevent this — it is a user discipline failure, not an AI output failure.
- **[[Risk Taxonomy#3.5 Tool / Security / Process Governance|Tool / Security / Process Governance]]: Shadow AI Risk (High)** — The tool used may be a personal consumer account rather than an approved organisation account. Consumer accounts may not have the organisation’s data protection settings, review workflow, or training opt-out protections.
- **[[Risk Taxonomy#3.6 Authority / Scope / Commitment|Authority / Scope / Commitment]]: Unauthorised Organisational Voice (High)** — For public posts, official notices, or social media, AI may generate content that sounds authoritative and represents the organisation’s position on matters not approved by leadership. Readers cannot distinguish AI-drafted wording from leadership-approved wording.
- **[[Risk Taxonomy#3.3 Context / Nuance / Compression|Context / Nuance / Compression]]: [[Human Review Risk Library#4.10 Instruction Drift|Instruction Drift]] (Medium)** — During repeated revisions, AI may move away from the original boundary, tone, source-only rule, or approval limit even after it initially acknowledged the instruction. The final message must be checked against the original instruction, not only the latest draft.

## 12. Other Mitigation

- Human review reduces risk, but it does not guarantee accuracy. Keep records and use approved tools.
- A review is only as strong as the sources checked. For legal, medical, financial, or regulatory claims, escalate to qualified review.
- A content review cannot fix the wrong tool, account, or data process. Fix the process before using the output.
- Human review should be done by someone who can approve the scope and commitments, not only someone checking spelling or grammar.
- If the communication depends on recent events, local history, internal tension, or audience sensitivity, use **Win.Win AI Blind Spot Guide** for deeper Blind Spot recognition and control selection. Surface or retrieve only the context needed for the current task. Use safe conversion or human-only handling where appropriate; stop, escalate, or do not use or record context that is unsuitable. Treat any recording or preservation as a separate governed decision.
