# Draft Volunteer Roles

## 1. What This Use Case Is

Use this page when you want AI to help with: **Draft volunteer onboarding materials and role descriptions**.

- Common users: **Church, nonprofit**.
- Approval level: **Generally approved**.
- Human review needed: **Yes — before distribution**.
- Use the AI output to help you draft or check the work. Do not treat it as the final decision.

---

## 2. What This Could Cost You

A volunteer-role draft may create confusion about duties, supervision, minimum commitments, payment or expenses, authority, safeguarding, or legal status if it is used without checking the organisation’s actual arrangement and current applicable requirements. The result may be an unintended commitment, an unsuitable role boundary, a dispute, or a need for qualified HR or legal review after the role has already been offered.

---

## 3. How To Review This Output

Use the checks below before approving the AI output. Do not approve it just because it reads well.

---

### 3.1 Review Setup

1. Review the actual arrangement and every stated requirement: duties, hours, attendance, supervision, accountability, training, authority, safeguarding, payment, expenses, and exit arrangements.
2. Compare the role description with the organisation’s approved volunteer policy and real operating practice. Identify any term that may create an unintended employment, payment, contractual, authority, or safeguarding obligation.
3. Remove or qualify language that promises a status, benefit, commitment, or power the organisation has not approved.
4. Obtain qualified HR or legal review before advertising where the role’s status, obligations, minimum commitments, or protections are unclear or material.

---

### 3.2 Before You Approve — One Check

> **Has the responsible owner confirmed that the duties, hours, supervision, training, payment or expenses, authority, safeguarding, and status match the actual arrangement — with qualified HR or legal review where any obligation or boundary is unclear?**

---

### 3.3 Reviewer Decision

After completing the task-specific review checks above, choose one outcome using [[Reviewer Decision Card]]:

- **Approve as is**
- **Correct then approve**
- **Stop and escalate**

Do not approve the output if the reviewer lacks the role, evidence, authority, competence, source access, context, or approved process needed for this task.

---

### 3.4 Review Methods for This Task

The review instructions below are customised for this task. They apply the reusable methods in [[Human Review Patterns]] to the actual sources, decisions, people, and consequences involved here.

- **Qualified legal / compliance review:** Check whether the role description creates employment-like terms, legal obligations, safeguarding duties, or other professional requirements.
  - Look for: Promises about pay, benefits, hours, discipline, status, confidentiality, or duties that require HR/legal/safeguarding review.
  - Timing: Before distribution or recruitment.
  - Reviewer: Qualified HR, legal, safeguarding, or relevant professional reviewer.

- **Scope, authority, and feasibility review:** Check that duties, time expectations, supervision, training, support, boundaries, and escalation routes match the real volunteer programme.
  - Look for: Unrealistic workload, unclear supervision, authority beyond the role, or missing support and duty-of-care arrangements.
  - Timing: Before the role is advertised or assigned.
  - Reviewer: Volunteer programme owner, supervisor, or authorised manager.

- **Human-grounded review:** Check the wording against the actual volunteer audience, relationship, power balance, accessibility, and trust context.
  - Look for: Language that pressures volunteers, hides optionality, or sounds contractual, dismissive, or inappropriate for the community.
  - Timing: Before distribution.
  - Reviewer: Volunteer coordinator or audience-aware reviewer.

---

## 4. When To Escalate Instead of Approve

- Escalate before use if the output affects legal, HR, financial, safeguarding, medical, regulatory, public-facing, or high-impact decisions and the reviewer does not have the authority, competence, context, source access, or approved process needed to confirm safe use.
- Escalate legal, regulatory, employment, PDPO, contract, compliance, or rights/obligation issues unless the reviewer is explicitly authorised and competent to confirm the matter under the approved process.
- Escalate when the source itself is unclear, outdated, missing, or conflicts with another approved source.

---

## 5. Before You Prompt

Before using AI for this task, check these basics:
- The purpose is clear.
- The input does not contain personal or confidential data that should not be entered into the tool.
- A responsible person can check the AI output before it is used.
- The task does not ask AI to make the decision, provide professional advice, or act as the final authority.

- Confirm that this task fits the approval level: **Generally approved**.
- Confirm that the planned reviewer can complete the required human review: **Yes — before distribution**.

---

## 6. AI Blind Spot Focus

**Risk focus:** Relationship and Trust Context Gap

**Prompt focus:** Before prompting, give the AI the role purpose, supervision structure, time expectations, boundaries, safeguarding/data considerations, approval owner, and what the role is not authorised to do. Ask it to flag assumptions about trust, responsibility, authority, and volunteer-versus-staff boundaries.

**Human review focus:** The reviewer should check whether the role description fits real volunteer capacity, past experience, informal trust boundaries, and whether it accidentally creates employment-like obligations or unsafe authority.

**Deeper Blind Spot review:** Follow the [[How To Use This Guide#6.1 Win.Win AI Blind Spot Guide|Win.Win AI Blind Spot Guide route]] when the role depends on trust, safeguarding, relationship history, role boundaries, past incidents, or informal knowledge about who should or should not hold the role.

**Do not rely only on:** role-description wording alone. The real risk may be trust, relationship, authority, or obligation, not grammar.

## 8. Main Risks

- **[[Risk Taxonomy#3.9 Legal / Regulatory / Compliance|Legal / Regulatory / Compliance]]: Implied Employment or Contractual Terms (High)** — A detailed volunteer role may create ambiguity about status, minimum commitments, supervision, payment, benefits, authority, performance expectations, or exit rights. The title “volunteer” does not by itself resolve those questions. Compare the draft with the real arrangement and current applicable requirements, and obtain qualified HR or legal review where the role may create material obligations.

---

## 9. Other Risks To Watch

- **[[Risk Taxonomy#3.11 AI Blind Spot / Context Risks|AI Blind Spot / Context Risks]]: Relationship and Trust Context Gap — related review risk: [[Human Review Risk Library#4.14 Unwritten Context Gap|Unwritten Context Gap]] (High)** — AI may draft a neutral-looking volunteer role without knowing trust level, past conduct, safeguarding concerns, informal boundaries, or community history that should shape the role.
- **[[Risk Taxonomy#3.9 Legal / Regulatory / Compliance|Legal / Regulatory / Compliance]]: Missing Protection and Duty Elements (High)** — AI may omit safeguarding, health and safety, supervision, confidentiality, insurance, complaints, or other protections required by the organisation’s actual role, current policy, contracts, or applicable requirements. Use approved sources and qualified review for roles involving vulnerable people or material responsibility.
- **[[Risk Taxonomy#3.2 Source Grounding / Evidence|Source Grounding / Evidence]]: Unrealistic Role Expectations (Low)** — AI may draft aspirational role descriptions that do not match the organisation’s actual capacity, supervision resources, or volunteer time. Volunteers recruited against inflated descriptions disengage or fail expectations.

## 11. Other Mitigation

- Ordinary human review is not enough. Final reliance requires qualified professional judgement and current authoritative sources.
- A review is only as strong as the sources checked. For legal, medical, financial, or regulatory claims, escalate to qualified review.
