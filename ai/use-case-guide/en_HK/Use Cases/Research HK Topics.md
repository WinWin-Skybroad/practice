# Research HK Topics

## 1. What This Use Case Is

Use this page when you want AI to help with: **Research Hong Kong-specific topics**.

- Common users: **Church, nonprofit, SME**.
- Approval level: **Use with caution**.
- Human review needed: **Yes — verify against official HK sources**.
- Use the AI output to help you draft or check the work. Do not treat it as the final decision.

---

## 2. What This Could Cost You

An HK-focused research answer may rely on an outdated policy position, miss Chinese-language official material, or mix Hong Kong and overseas assumptions. If the answer is treated as current without checking official sources and dates, the organisation may plan, apply, communicate, or decide on the wrong basis.

---

## 3. How To Review This Output

Use the checks below before approving the AI output. Do not approve it just because it reads well.

---

### 3.1 Review Setup

1. List every named policy, scheme, eligibility criterion, and official body in the output.
2. Go directly to the relevant government or regulatory website for each one.
3. Check the effective or last-updated date of any policy or guideline.
4. If the official website does not confirm the information, treat the AI output as unverified.

---

### 3.2 Before You Approve — One Check

> **Has every policy position, scheme requirement, and official guideline in this output been verified directly against the current official government or regulatory website — not from the AI output and not from a general search result?**

---

### 3.3 Risk-Specific Review Checks

These checks adapt the minimum review obligations in [[Human Review Risk Library]] to this task.

- **[[Human Review Risk Library#4.23 Local or jurisdiction-specific blind spot|Local or jurisdiction-specific blind spot]]:** Check every material claim against current Hong Kong official or authoritative sources and the actual institution, sector, locality, audience, and period. Verify bilingual terminology and reject Mainland China, overseas, or generic assumptions that do not apply.

---

### 3.4 Reviewer Decision

After completing the task-specific review checks above, choose one outcome using [[Reviewer Decision Card]]:

- **Approve as is**
- **Correct then approve**
- **Stop and escalate**

Do not approve the output if the reviewer lacks the role, evidence, authority, competence, source access, context, or approved process needed for this task.

---

### 3.5 Review Methods for This Task

The review instructions below are customised for this task. They apply the reusable methods in [[Human Review Patterns]] to the actual sources, decisions, people, and consequences involved here.

- **Source and fact verification:** Verify material claims against current official Hong Kong sources, local institutions, primary records, or reliable Hong Kong evidence.
  - Look for: Mainland China or overseas defaults, outdated Hong Kong policy, unsupported local claims, and sources that do not apply to the named sector or period.
  - Timing: Before the research is shared or relied on.
  - Reviewer: Hong Kong subject owner, researcher, or source-checking reviewer.

- **Bilingual / localisation review:** Check English and Chinese names, official terminology, organisation titles, legal/regulatory terms, and Hong Kong language usage against authoritative local sources.
  - Look for: Mainland terminology, script mixing, mistranslated institution names, or English and Chinese terms referring to different concepts.
  - Timing: Before publication or external use.
  - Reviewer: Qualified bilingual reviewer familiar with Hong Kong terminology.

- **Human-grounded review:** Check whether the research fits the actual Hong Kong institution, sector, community, locality, audience, and intended use rather than a generic description of Hong Kong.
  - Look for: A factually plausible answer that misses local institutional practice, current context, or the real audience affected.
  - Timing: Before decision use.
  - Reviewer: Local subject owner, institutional owner, or person with relevant Hong Kong experience.

---

## 4. When To Escalate Instead of Approve

- Escalate before use if the output affects legal, HR, financial, safeguarding, medical, regulatory, public-facing, or high-impact decisions and the reviewer does not have the authority, competence, context, source access, or approved process needed to confirm safe use.
- Escalate legal, medical, financial, technical, or public translation when the reviewer is not qualified or authorised to confirm the specialist meaning.
- Escalate legal, regulatory, employment, PDPO, contract, compliance, or rights/obligation issues unless the reviewer is explicitly authorised and competent to confirm the matter under the approved process.
- Escalate whenever personal data, HR, pastoral, theological, medical, financial, HKID, beneficiary, or confidential information is involved and the reviewer does not have the authority, context, source access, or approved process needed to confirm safe use.

---

## 5. Before You Prompt

Before using AI for this task, check these basics:
- The purpose is clear.
- The input does not contain personal or confidential data that should not be entered into the tool.
- A responsible person can check the AI output before it is used.
- The task does not ask AI to make the decision, provide professional advice, or act as the final authority.

- Confirm that this task fits the approval level: **Use with caution**.
- Confirm that the planned reviewer can complete the required human review: **Yes — verify against official HK sources**.

---

## 6. AI Blind Spot Focus

**Risk focus:** Local Institutional Context Gap

**Prompt focus:** Before prompting, give the AI the HK-specific question, jurisdiction, date sensitivity, preferred official sources, Chinese/English source requirements, organisation type, and intended use. Ask it to mark HK-specific assumptions and items requiring official-source verification.

**Human review focus:** Verify against current HK official or authoritative sources. Check whether AI imported non-HK assumptions, missed Chinese-language material, or treated a local institutional issue as a generic topic.

**Deeper Blind Spot review:** Follow the [[How To Use This Guide#6.1 Win.Win AI Blind Spot Guide|Win.Win AI Blind Spot Guide route]] when the answer may depend on Hong Kong-specific institutions, Chinese-language sources, local practice, current government context, or jurisdiction-specific meaning.

**Do not rely only on:** generic expertise. HK-specific work may turn on local institutions, bilingual sources, current practice, and official wording.

## 8. Main Risks

- **[[Risk Taxonomy#3.8 Bilingual / Localisation / HK Context|Bilingual / Localisation / HK Context]]: HK Coverage and Currency Gap (High)** — HK-specific policy, institutional, legal, and regulatory material may be documented across English and Chinese official sources and may change over time. AI may provide incomplete, outdated, mistranslated, or cross-jurisdictional coverage without signalling the gap. Treat HK-specific research as a starting point for checking current official sources in the relevant languages, not as the final answer.

---

## 9. Other Risks To Watch

- **[[Risk Taxonomy#3.11 AI Blind Spot / Context Risks|AI Blind Spot / Context Risks]]: Local Institutional Context Gap — related review risk: [[Human Review Risk Library#4.23 Local or jurisdiction-specific blind spot|Local or jurisdiction-specific blind spot]] (High)** — AI may know general concepts but miss HK-specific institutional practice, bilingual official terminology, current government guidance, or sector-specific interpretation.
- **[[Risk Taxonomy#3.9 Legal / Regulatory / Compliance|Legal / Regulatory / Compliance]]: Mainland China Default — related review risk: [[Human Review Risk Library#4.23 Local or jurisdiction-specific blind spot|Local or jurisdiction-specific blind spot]] (High)** — Regulatory, legal, and cultural content may introduce PRC terminology, assumptions, or institutional references, especially when the query is in Simplified Chinese or when Hong Kong context is not explicit. Hong Kong and Mainland China have different legal, governance, and institutional contexts.
- **[[Risk Taxonomy#3.4 Privacy / Confidentiality / Data Boundary|Privacy / Confidentiality / Data Boundary]]: Outdated HK Policy and Legislation (High)** — HK ordinances and policies change with some frequency. AI training data may not reflect recent amendments to ordinances, new PCPD guidance, updated SFC or HKMA requirements, or changes to government structures.
- **[[Risk Taxonomy#3.8 Bilingual / Localisation / HK Context|Bilingual / Localisation / HK Context]]: English-Chinese Terminology Mismatch (Medium)** — Official HK terminology in English and Traditional Chinese differs from both UK English equivalents and Mainland Chinese equivalents. AI may use the wrong term and the reviewer may not recognise the error.

## 11. Other Mitigation

- Fluent text can hide meaning drift; bilingual review must compare source and target, not just read the target.
- If sensitive data was already entered into an unapproved tool, output review cannot undo exposure. Escalate according to privacy / incident process.
